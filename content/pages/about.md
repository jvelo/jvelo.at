I'm a freelance software product engineer and tech lead based in Toulouse, France.

<p class="about-resume">For my work history, see my <a href="/resume">résumé</a>.</p>

I published my first websites as a teenager in the early 2000s, using Notepad and FileZilla. 
Back then, the name of the game was using `View Source` to try and copy some
style or effect from other people's websites. 

<figure>
<img src="/images/notepad.png" alt="How it felt back then" />

</figure>

I went on to make it my job: first as a software developer, then in tech leadership and product 
work. My first actual job in software was an internship at Frog Navigation Systems, a Dutch company 
making AGVs (now part of Oceaneering Mobile Robotics). That's where I made my first open source 
contribution, a small patch on JacORB. That was in 2006. Funnily, I didn't hear back about it until 
about six years later, when it was merged. By then, open source had already shaped my early career: 
I had joined [XWiki](https://www.xwiki.com), the company behind the eponymous 
open source collaborative software suite.

At XWiki, I got to discover the nascent world of [professional open source](https://web.archive.org/web/20190821183922/https://developer.jboss.org/blogs/marcf/2005/05/27/is-open-source-financially-viable). 
The project followed in spirit the Apache Software Foundation way of building software, while the 
company served customers relying on the platform. This was the early "Web 2.0" era, and the 
acronyms of the times were AJAX, SOA or the forgotten DHTML. I was a core committer on XWiki 
projects during my time there, contributing to frontend and backend features.

In 2011, I joined forces with my friend [Louis](https://blog-espritdesign.com/rubriques/metier-deco-design-archi/rencontre-louis-beziau-designer-et-president-de-lagence-les-sismo-84721). First, we created a social network for wine enthusiasts that didn't take off.
We were both new to entrepreneurship and naively thought that if we built it, they would come. We 
found out it doesn't work that way. In the meantime, we created a small web and UX shop. I 
also had an itch to build an open
source project of my own — something I could lead from the ground up. That became
[Mayocat Shop](/mayocat-shop), an open source e-commerce and marketplace platform. We deployed
quite a few websites on the platform, some of which are still running on it a dozen years later.

In late 2016, I joined Agricool. It was my first job in agriculture, though I had long been 
interested in biology and controlled environments. That was the beginning of a ten-year arc in 
AgTech. At Agricool, I learned how a VC-funded startup operates while leading an international 
team of up to eight software and DevOps engineers. We built software for a company 
dealing with a stubbornly physical reality: crops, machines, production, and day-to-day 
operations. Our time there is a story of its own.

Fast-forward to 2021, I co-founded [Orius](https://www.orius.co/) with two colleagues from the 
Agricool gang. We took a different approach to indoor farming: rather than competing on low-margin 
fruit and vegetable commodities, we used controlled-environment agriculture to optimize 
phytochemical profiles of plants for cosmetics, supplements and pharmaceuticals. We created our own controlled 
environment technology, which pushes the envelope of agronomical research and serves as a reference 
design for the French space agency's work on Moon and Mars habitats. I 
designed and developed [BiomeOS](/biomeos), the software runtime and orchestration platform used in 
research and production. I also built several other software products, including the control 
software for a [gravity simulator](/gravilab), and led the company's marketing and branding efforts.

At Orius, software ultimately served biology, chemistry, and agriculture. I left in 
August 2026 wanting to work again on products where software itself is make-or-break, and where 
my expertise lines up directly with the stakes of the project. I embraced coding agents early, 
and their progress has made it an especially exciting time to build software. Beyond their 
effect on how software is made, I'm interested in building AI-native products and 
bringing AI capabilities to existing ones. I also wanted time to ship some products of my own,
including [Typebar](/typebar) and [Savannah](/savannah).

Onward.
