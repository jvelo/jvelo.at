I'm a freelance software product engineer and tech lead based in Toulouse, France.

I published my first websites as a teenager in the early 2000's, using Notepad and FileZilla. 
Back then, the name of the game was using `View Source` to try and copy some
style or effect from other people's websites. 

<figure>
<img src="/images/notepad.png" alt="How it felt back then" />

keyboard tray</figcaption>
</figure>

I've since made it my job, first as a software developer, then into tech leadership and product 
work. My first "real work" was at a Dutch company making AGVs, Frog Navigation Systems (now 
part of Oceaneering mobile robotics) as an intern. That's where I made my first open source 
contribution, a small patch on JacORB. This was 2006. Funnily, I didn't hear back about it until 
about 6 years later, when it was merged. In the meantime, my early career would be marked by 
open source, since I had joined [XWiki](https://www.xwiki.com), the editor of the eponymous 
open source collaborative software suite.

<figure>
<img src="/images/jacorb-patch.png" alt="Thanks for the patch" />
<figcaption>The first patch</figcaption>

At XWiki, I got to discover the nascent world of [professional open source](https://web.archive.org/web/20190821183922/https://developer.jboss.org/blogs/marcf/2005/05/27/is-open-source-financially-viable). 
The project follows in spirit the Apache Software Foundation way of building software (though 
not affiliated with the ASF), while the corporation served customers relying on the platform. 
This was early "Web 2.0" era, and the acronyms of the times were AJAX, SOA or the forgotten 
DHTML. I was a core committer on XWiki projects during my time there, contributing to frontend 
and backend features.

In 2011, I joined forces with my friend [Louis](https://blog-espritdesign.com/rubriques/metier-deco-design-archi/rencontre-louis-beziau-designer-et-president-de-lagence-les-sismo-84721). First, we created a social network for wine enthusiasts, that didn't take off.
We were both new to entrepreneurship and naively thought that if we built it they would come. We 
found out it doesn't work that way. In the meantime we've created a small web and UX shop. I 
also had an itch to build an open
source project of my own — something I could lead from the ground up. That became
[Mayocat Shop](/mayocat-shop), an open source e-commerce and marketplace platform. We've deployed
quite a few websites on the platform, some of them still run on it a dozen years later.

In late 2016, I joined Agricool. While not really my field, I cultivated a long-time 
interest in biology and controlled environment agriculture through discussions with a friend who
works in urban agriculture. This would be the beginning of a 10 year arc in the industry. At 
Agricool I learned how a VC-funded startup operates, and how to grow a team of software 
developers. My time there is a story of its own.

Fast-forward 2021, I co-founded [Orius](https://www.orius.co/) with two colleagues from the 
Agricool gang. We took a different approach to indoor farming: rather than competing on low-margin 
fruit and vegetable commodities, we leverage the technology to optimize phytochemical profiles of 
plants for cosmetics, supplements and pharmaceuticals. We created our own controlled 
environment tech that pushes the envelope of agronomical research, and is a reference design 
for the French space agency when exploring the possibilities of Moon and Mars habitats. I've 
developed [BiomeOS](/biomeos), the software runtime and orchestration platform used in research and 
production.
